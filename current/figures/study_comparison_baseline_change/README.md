## What each point means

Match Study 1 and Study 2 by population, age category (3–9 or Adult), measure and role. For each Study 2 condition, subtract the same matching Study 1 baseline:

- Intentional change = Study 2 Intentional proportion Yes − Study 1 proportion Yes.
- Accidental change = Study 2 Accidental proportion Yes − Study 1 proportion Yes.

Average available Bike/Mirror binary answers within participant × measure × role before group averaging. Yes=1, No=0; both yes contributes 1 to both roles, and neither contributes 0 to both. Missingness stays question-specific. No relative-attribution scoring, baseline division or endorsement-based exclusion is used. Existing inclusion and repeat-session choices are unchanged.

Columns are Caused, Lexical, Fault and Punish; rows are Distal and Proximal. Purple solid lines/filled circles show Intentional; teal dashed lines/open triangles show Accidental. Child age means are connected; adults are separate endpoints. The y-axis is **Change from baseline (%)**, from -50% to 50%, with zero meaning the Study 1 baseline. Percentage ticks represent percentage-point differences: 10% means ten percentage points more Yes responses, not a ten-percent relative increase. All observed values fit (range -38.55 to 32.40 points). Four CI ends extend beyond the displayed range and are truncated at the panel boundary without arrowheads; the complete bounds remain in the CSV. Study 1 is represented by the zero reference, without a third trajectory.

## Intervals and interpretation

Pointwise 95% participant-bootstrap intervals include uncertainty in both studies. Within each age/measure/role cell, independently resample participant means in Study 1 and in each Study 2 condition 5,000 times, using a fixed seed. Reuse the same Study 1 bootstrap draw for both contrasts, subtract the bootstrap means, and take percentile limits. Do not subtract raw-mean interval endpoints.

This is a descriptive comparison between study samples, not within-person change or an isolated causal estimate of adding intention information. The analysis treats samples as independent; retained codes do not overlap, but different anonymization schemes could conceal shared people. Cohort/deployment differences and household dependence remain limitations.

The child samples are the existing uncapped descriptive snapshots (211 Study 1; 406 Study 2). Adult compares Study 1 N=100 with the selected Study 2 pilot `exp2_new_pilot-responses.csv` (20 Intentional; 21 Accidental). The preregistered main Study 2 adult dataset is still missing. Original analysis scripts and other scientific handoff gaps remain unresolved.

## Age-pooled Study 2 Distal-minus-Proximal gap summary

[Summary PNG](study2_distal_proximal_gap_summary.png) · [Vector PDF](study2_distal_proximal_gap_summary.pdf) · [Exact eight gaps and CIs](study2_distal_proximal_gap_summary_values.csv)

Pool Study 2 children aged 3–9 (206 Intentional, 200 Accidental), retaining the same inclusion decisions and excluding the adult pilot. Average Yes=1/No=0 across available scenarios within each child, measure and role, then compute that child's `gap_pp = 100 × (Distal mean − Proximal mean)`. Average these paired gaps across children within measure and condition. Both/neither remain included. A gap requires an available mean for both roles; all current children have both for every measure. Source means in the CSV use these same paired contributors.

The single panel shows one Intentional filled purple circle and one Accidental open teal triangle per measure, joined by a thin neutral connector within that measure only. Zero means equal endorsement; positive values favor Distal and negative values favor Proximal. The y-axis is **Distal − Proximal (percentage points)**. Percentage tick labels denote absolute percentage-point differences, not relative percent changes. The range rounds outward to 25-point steps and includes every interval endpoint.

The 95% intervals come from 5,000 fixed-seed bootstrap replicates of participant rows within condition. Each child's four paired role gaps stay together; the roles are never resampled independently. The eight-row CSV includes eligible/paired Ns, both source proportions, `gap_pp`, `lower_pp` and `upper_pp` without display rounding. The means are participant-weighted, not equally age-weighted, and are not age-standardized. No extra summary builds are created.

Intentional produces a more positive gap for every measure. The Intentional-minus-Accidental separation is greatest for Punish (+39.60 points), followed by Lexical (+27.95), Fault (+22.88) and Caused (+16.20). Fault has a large Intentional distal preference, but its between-condition widening is smaller than Lexical's. Both Lexical gaps are negative. The pointwise intervals do not formally test differences in intentionality-related widening across measures. Unequal age composition and the interim sample remain limitations.

This figure and its CSV replace the prior Study 2 two-panel condition-mean summary. Raw Study 1/2 plots, the baseline-change figure and the Single Cause outputs remain separate and unchanged.

## Files and reproduction

The full figure is 16 × 9 inches; PNGs are 3840 × 2160 pixels. All PDF figures use vector graphics. Build 00 contains all eight empty panels, axes, zero lines and the legend. Builds 01–04 cumulatively reveal Caused, Lexical, Fault and Punish for both roles and conditions. Build 04 equals the full figure. Geometry, scales and previously revealed data stay fixed.

`baseline_change_plotted_means.csv` has one row per age × measure × role × condition: 128 rows. It includes `baseline_n`, `study2_n`, `baseline`, `study2_yes`, `change`, and bootstrap `lower`/`upper`. These are proportions; `change_pp`, `lower_pp` and `upper_pp` give percentage points. CSV values are saved without display rounding. The Rmd also prints a compact source-mean/change table and checks every value against an independent calculation from binary response rows.
