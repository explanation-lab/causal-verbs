## Scores and samples

Responses retain ordinary Yes=1, No=0 coding, including both/neither. Average
available scenarios within participant first. The existing paired-role scoring
is reused; the included Study 1/2 samples have complete scenario-level scores.

- Causal-role differentiation = `((Caused Distal - Caused Proximal) + (Lexical Proximal - Lexical Distal)) / 2`.
- Distal responsibility preference = `Fault or Punish Distal - Proximal`.
- The developmental plot's colored lines instead show `Intentional - Accidental` differences in Yes proportions for Single Cause or Chain: Distal.

Multiply proportion differences by 100 to obtain **percentage points**; a tick
of 25% denotes a 25-percentage-point difference, not a relative percent change.
Higher differentiation means the expected combination of distal Caused and
proximal Lexical endorsement. Higher responsibility preference means greater
distal than proximal endorsement.

Study 1 relationship models use all **211 eligible unique children ages 3–9**.
Study 2 models use all **406 eligible unique children ages 3–9** (200 Accidental,
206 Intentional). No adults enter either model. The developmental endpoints
retain the established samples: Study 1 adults N=100; Single Cause children
N=96 and adults N=32; Study 2 children N=406 and the selected adult pilot N=41.
The Study 2 main adult dataset remains missing. All inclusion and pilot caveats
in the [workflow guide](../../../code/R/README.md) still apply.

## Age adjustment and uncertainty

Fit separate ordinary least-squares models for Fault and Punish:

```r
# Study 1, children only
responsibility ~ differentiation + age

# Study 2, children only; Accidental is the reference condition
responsibility ~ differentiation * intention + age
```

Age is **numeric age in years**, with an additive linear effect. This follows
the requested formulas; the earlier categorical `age_group` analyses remain
unchanged. In the implementation, outcomes are in percentage points,
differentiation is scaled to 25-point units, and age is centered at the study's
child mean (Study 1: 5.9573 years; Study 2: 6.0320 years). These changes of units
and origin do not change model fits.

Fitted curves average over each study's observed child-age distribution, using
the **same pooled distribution for both Study 2 conditions**. Additive linear
age makes this equivalent to prediction at the mean age. Curves stay within
the observed differentiation range of the relevant study/condition.
Faint points are raw participant scores, with display-only jitter of at most
1.5 percentage points. The fit is not calculated from jittered data.

All new model CIs use **5,000 whole-participant bootstrap replicates**. Resample
within age for Study 1, and within age × intention for Study 2, retaining each
child's differentiation and both outcomes together. Original stratum sizes and
the prediction age distribution remain fixed. Seeds are `20260920 + 26` and
`20260920 + 27`. Bands show pointwise 95% percentile intervals for the fitted
mean, not individual prediction intervals or simultaneous confidence bands.

Developmental V2 reuses all 30 V1 means and CIs exactly, plus the established
Study 1 differentiation intervals at ages 7–9. Only the six new Chain: Distal
age-7–9 means/intervals require additional computation; both independent
condition samples are bootstrapped at each age, preserving the paired
Fault/Punish outcomes (5,000 replicates, seed `20260920 + 25`). Every interval
is displayed in full; no confidence bound is clipped.

## Age-adjusted results

All estimates and confidence limits below are **percentage points**. Slopes and
interactions describe a **25-point increase** in causal differentiation.
Accidental is the reference, so the Study 2 differentiation main effect is
its Accidental simple slope. The intention main effect is Intentional minus
Accidental **at zero differentiation**, not an unconditional average effect.

| Study | Effect | Fault estimate [95% CI] | Punish estimate [95% CI] |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Study 1 | Differentiation, per 25 points | 14.14 [9.87, 18.33] | 9.48 [4.78, 14.08] |
| Study 2 | Differentiation main effect / Accidental slope, per 25 points | 7.17 [1.80, 12.22] | 2.61 [-2.14, 7.52] |
| Study 2 | Intentional simple slope, per 25 points | 6.86 [3.15, 10.68] | 8.00 [4.43, 11.74] |
| Study 2 | Intention effect at zero differentiation | 22.81 [9.17, 37.21] | 31.23 [17.01, 45.42] |
| Study 2 | Differentiation × intention interaction, per 25 points | -0.31 [-6.55, 6.09] | 5.39 [-0.48, 11.11] |

Study 1 shows positive age-adjusted relationships for both responsibility
judgments. In Study 2, the fault slopes are positive and nearly equal.
Punishment's estimated slope is larger under intention, but its interaction CI
includes zero: **these results do not establish a difference between punishment
slopes**. A positive slope in one condition and an interval containing zero in
the other is not itself evidence of an interaction. At zero differentiation,
Intentional judgments show more distal responsibility preference for both
outcomes, with both intention-effect CIs above zero.

These cross-sectional models cannot establish that differentiation causes
responsibility judgments, mediates development, or changes within a child.
The differentiation score is itself measured in the assigned condition; the
intention coefficient is a conditional association, not an unconditional
treatment effect. CIs are exploratory, unadjusted for multiple comparisons,
and assume independent participants; household dependence is not modeled.
Ongoing collection, preregistered cap/completion-order questions and all
unresolved scientific handoff gaps remain explicit in the repository.
